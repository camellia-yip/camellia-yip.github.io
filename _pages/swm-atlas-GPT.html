01 / Overview

A closer look beneath the cortical surface

Superficial white matter (SWM) supports communication between nearby cortical regions. Its short, curved trajectories and location near the gray-white matter boundary make it difficult to reconstruct and organize consistently.

This work builds a dedicated SWM atlas from whole-brain tractography and groups its pathways by the functional networks they connect.

  • Joint identification. Geometric and positional criteria are combined with a neural classifier to select plausible SWM clusters.
  • Two network scales. Hierarchical merging produces atlas versions for the Yeo 7- and 17-network schemes.
  • Complementary annotations. Meta-analytic associations and a structure-function uncertainty index support interpretation.

02 / Method

Identify, merge and annotate

The framework starts from high-resolution whole-brain tractography, identifies superficial pathways, then examines their network organization and structural-functional coherence.

  1. 01

    Reconstruct

    Combine deterministic and probabilistic tractography from HCP 7T diffusion MRI and organize streamlines into fine-grained clusters.

  2. 02

    Select & merge

    Combine geometry-based selection with a classifier trained on expert-informed SWM labels. Merge geometrically similar clusters using hierarchical clustering.

  3. 03

    Characterize

    Use network labels, Neurosynth annotations and TW-dFC from corresponding HCP 3T resting-state fMRI to describe the resulting atlas.

03 / Results

A finer map with two levels of organization

From thousands of candidates to a compact atlas

Hierarchical merging reduces 1,719 identified SWM clusters to 643 under Yeo 7, and 4,189 to 1,403 under Yeo 17. Both versions contain connections within and between cortical networks.

A descriptive view of structure-function coherence

TW-dFC and independent component analysis provide an uncertainty index for each cluster. The paper compares a strict single-component rule with a relaxed rule allowing two components; their distributions differ, while the two atlas versions show similar overall patterns.

The study also compares SWM track-density patterns with two independent high-quality diffusion MRI datasets. Within-cohort correspondence, external image-pattern similarity and functional annotations provide complementary views of the resource.

Interpreting the resource

This is a tractography-derived atlas. Its functional annotations are descriptive, and its transfer to other acquisition settings or populations requires dataset-specific validation.
