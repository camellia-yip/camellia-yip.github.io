01 / Overview

Studying variation in gendered self-perception

The study examines individual variation in gender identity alongside regional cortical morphology. It combines structural MRI with psychological, cognitive and socioeconomic measures from the Amsterdam Open MRI Collection (AOMIC).

Two self-report items capture how strongly participants identify as male and female. The analyses treat these ratings as separate continuous dimensions and examine associations within the dataset's recorded sex groups.

  • A dimensional question. Examine variation in identity ratings within a non-clinical population.
  • A multimodal analysis. Bring together brain morphology, personality, cognition and socioeconomic context.
  • An explicit statistical model. Investigate how reported associations relate under a structural equation model.

02 / Method

Linking brain measures with individual experience

The analysis uses AOMIC's preprocessed structural measures and available behavioral data, followed by subgroup correlations and exploratory modeling.

  1. 01

    Measure

    Use preprocessed cortical thickness, area, volume and curvature across 68 regions, alongside identity ratings and psychological measures.

  2. 02

    Associate

    Examine correlations within the recorded sex groups, including associations with cognition, personality and socioeconomic status.

  3. 03

    Model

    Use partial correlations and an exploratory structural equation model to examine relationships involving fusiform cortical thickness.

03 / Results

Regional associations and distinct statistical paths

Visual and medial temporal regions

Within the female group, higher feminine identity ratings were associated with lower cortical thickness in the left fusiform, lingual, parahippocampal and pericalcarine regions. The male-group analyses reported different associations involving visual regions, the supramarginal gyrus and insula, as summarized in Figure 2 and the paper's tables.

An exploratory model centered on fusiform thickness

The model considers associations involving the parahippocampal cortex, a pericalcarine-lingual route, and a separate socioeconomic-cognitive route. Several individual indirect terms were statistically significant; the reported total indirect association between feminine identity and fusiform thickness was not significant (p = 0.106).

Psychological and contextual associations

Reported correlations varied across the study groups. In the female group, feminine identity ratings were negatively associated with socioeconomic status and crystallized intelligence. In the male group, masculine and feminine identity ratings showed different association patterns with anxiety, neuroticism and behavioral inhibition.

Reading these findings

These are exploratory, cross-sectional associations in a young, non-clinical sample. The measures describe variation within this dataset; causal and broader population interpretations require additional evidence.
